DISCOVER THE SCIENCE OF PLANT LIFE

Explore plant diversity, structure, physiology and the important role of plants in sustaining life across terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems.

Biological Science

Foundations of Botanical Science

Botany is the biological study of plants—from microscopic unicellular algae and bryophytes to complex vascular angiosperms. In the Intermediate BiPC program, Botany lays the structural and physiological groundwork for all life sciences.

Through systematic exploration of plant anatomy, biochemical energetics (photosynthesis and cellular respiration), genetics, and molecular biotechnology, students gain profound insights into primary producers that support planetary biodiversity.

Laboratory practicals reinforce theoretical classroom teaching through live section cutting, compound microscope examination, and botanical taxonomy documentation.

Illustrative Syllabus

Key Learning Areas in Botany

Illustrative topic modules covered across Intermediate First and Second Year Botany curricula.

1. Diversity in Plant Kingdom

Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms evolutionary characteristics.

2. Plant Classification

Taxonomic hierarchies, binomial nomenclature, Bentham & Hooker system, and floral formulas.

3. Plant Morphology

Root, stem, leaf modifications, inflorescence types, flower structures, fruits, and seeds.

4. Plant Anatomy

Meristematic and permanent tissues, vascular bundles, secondary growth in dicot stems and roots.

5. Cell Biology

Plant cell ultrastructure, cell wall, chloroplasts, vacuoles, mitosis, and meiosis phases.

6. Plant Physiology

Water potential, osmosis, transpiration pull, xylem sap ascent, and mineral nutrition requirements.

7. Photosynthesis

Light reactions, Calvin cycle ($C_3$), Hatch-Slack pathway ($C_4$), CAM, and photorespiration.

8. Respiration in Plants

Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport system (ETS), oxidative phosphorylation, and ATP yield.

9. Plant Reproduction

Microsporogenesis, megasporogenesis, pollination mechanisms, double fertilization, and embryogeny.

10. Plant Genetics

Mendelian inheritance principles, dihybrid crosses, incomplete dominance, and chromosomal basis of heredity.

11. Plant Ecology

Ecosystem trophic structure, ecological pyramids, plant succession, and environmental adaptations.

12. Plant Biotechnology

Tissue culture techniques, recombinant DNA tools in crop improvement, and transgenic plant applications.

Syllabus Notice: These topic modules are illustrative for typical Intermediate Board programs. Exact chapter titles and weightages are guided by current official Board circulars.
Microscopic Precision

Microscopic Study & Tissue Observation

Under high-precision compound microscopes, students observe stomatal mechanisms, chloroplast arrangements, epidermal peels, and cross-sections of monocot and dicot stems.

Developing the tactile skill of taking razor-thin transverse sections (T.S.), gentle safranin staining, and bubble-free mounting fosters genuine scientific discipline.

Laboratory Practical

Botany Laboratory Activities

Our Botany laboratory sessions bridge textbook terminology with hands-on specimen investigations:

  • Plant Specimen Observation: Identifying live botanical specimens across Fabaceae, Solanaceae, and Liliaceae families.
  • Floral Dissection: Isolating calyx, corolla, androecium, and gynoecium to construct accurate floral diagrams.
  • Physiological Demonstrations: Ganong’s potometer for transpiration rates and paper chromatography of leaf pigments.
  • Herbarium & Record Maintenance: Scientific preservation and systematic board record compilation.
